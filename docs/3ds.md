# 3DS games

[Back to the README](../README.md)

X, Y, Omega Ruby, Alpha Sapphire, Sun, Moon, Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon play best natively, with [3DSrecomp+Zakuro](#3dsrecompzakuro) below: it comes with nativeMons and is the recommended way. A 3DS emulator is the optional fallback, for encrypted dumps or when you prefer one. Turn on **3DS games** under **Emu** on the home screen (or in Settings), then install **AzaharPlus** there: the recommended emulator, Azahar with extras, which also opens encrypted dumps. Plain **Azahar** is there too, as a backup for when AzaharPlus misbehaves. Nothing downloads until you pick one. Each is built from our own fork of it, is checked against a pinned checksum before it's put in place, and lives in `emulators\`, which a launcher update never touches; removing one never touches your saves. When a newer build of your emulator is ready, Play asks whether to update first; **Do not remind me of this update** stops the question for that one update.

- **ROMs:** `.3ds` or `.cci` dumps of your own cartridges (GodMode9 on your 3DS), played where they are, never copied. No games, keys or system files are included, and our AzaharPlus build leaves out its downloader for Nintendo's system files.
- **Saves** belong to the launcher, in `state\3ds\saves`, not to an emulator: each play hands the save to the emulator you chose and takes it back after, with a backup before and after, so you can switch emulators. A save that fails its check after a session is never kept as the one in use: it goes to the backups, and your last good save stays. Save slots work as for the other games, and each slot keeps its own save states.
- **Controls, as on Fulldex:** every connected controller is mapped by the letters on its buttons, and a second profile, "Keyboard (Fulldex keys)", has Fulldex's keys (the emulator binds each button to one device, so pick that profile in its Controls to play on the keyboard). Change either there. The launcher also sets Vulkan with background shader building (the first minutes of a game are smoother each time you play), your **Game options** volume, and no "are you sure" on close.
- **My Collection** counts their Pokédex, and **Move Pokemon** moves box Pokémon in and out of them (Gen 6 and 7, the same rules as the other games). After a move, that save slot's save states go to its backups, so loading an old one can't undo the move. Export and import of 3DS saves aren't there yet.

## 3DSrecomp+Zakuro

Recommended over the emulators for the games it runs: they play more smoothly. A native engine for X, Y, Omega Ruby, Alpha Sapphire, Sun, Moon, Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon: the ones Zakuro lists as playable with recompiled code, plus Moon, which Zakuro doesn't list yet but which we tested ourselves (its code is almost all Sun's, and it plays the same). [3dsrecomp](https://github.com/fearkov/3dsrecomp) turns your game's code into C and compiles it into a library that [Zakuro](https://github.com/fearkov/zakuro) runs, in place of emulating the CPU. It comes with nativeMons, in `engines\zakuro`, and needs a decrypted dump.

- **Built on your PC, once per game.** No game code is in nativeMons or its downloads. When start-up finds new 3DS games, it gets them ready behind the splash (Zig, the checks), then asks once: **Set up in the background** fixes any header that needs it and builds them one by one while you keep using the launcher, with a banner showing how far along. Play on a game still building says so and offers the emulator for that time. Builds go into `state\3ds\recomp` and stay on your PC. About 10 to 20 minutes per game on a typical PC, at the lowest priority; **Settings > 3DS build speed** picks Fast (all but one processor thread) or Quiet (half). Esc or B stops a build. A sister game (X after Y, and so on) builds faster, from the parts they share. The first build downloads Zig, the C compiler it uses (about 100 MB, checked against a pinned checksum).
- **A decrypted dump whose header still says encrypted** (some decrypting tools leave it that way, often for Omega Ruby, Alpha Sapphire and the Sun and Moon games) is refused by Zakuro and 3dsrecomp. The game's page, and the build itself, offer **Fix 3DS header**: it checks the game really is decrypted, then changes 2 bytes of the header as GodMode9 does, keeping the original bytes in `state\3ds\header-backups`. **Undo 3DS header fix** puts them back. Emulators play the dump the same either way.
- **Controls, as on Fulldex:** Fulldex's keys (Settings > Controls lists them all, and the guide button's overlay too), and a controller by the letters on its buttons; hold its right trigger (or Tab) to fast forward, up to 4 times the speed. Change either in Zakuro's menu (Esc), under Controls; the launcher sets the controller only once. While the guide overlay is open, the controller doesn't reach the game.
- **Screen:** it opens on the launcher's screen, with the big top screen and a small bottom one (F9 or Zakuro's menu changes that, and it stays). **Game options** reach it too: volume, fullscreen, and Smooth scaling (off: sharp pixels); in a window, it's sized to fit the screen.
- **Gets faster as you play:** Zakuro notes the places in a game's code its build didn't cover. After a session that found enough of them, the launcher rebuilds that game quietly in the background (lowest priority, half the processor threads, usually under a minute from the cache), with a small banner showing its progress; the next play uses it. Closing the launcher stops it, and the next session starts it again.
- **Sharing helps everyone:** after such a session the launcher also asks whether to share that list (addresses only, no game code, nothing about you): **Yes**, **No** (asked again after more new findings) or **Never ask again** (Settings > Ask to share Zakuro findings turns it back on; the game's page can share any time). Yes opens a form on this repository and shows you the file to attach. Once checked, the list goes into the shared lists, which the launcher downloads at start-up and every few hours, with no update needed. A built game whose shared list grew is rebuilt quietly to cover it.
- **Save states** (F5 saves, F8 loads) belong to the save slot, like the emulators' do. Zakuro's and the emulators' can't load in each other, so the slot keeps both kinds.
- **60 fps (Zakuro)** on a game's page turns on Zakuro's community code for X, Y, Omega Ruby, Sun, Ultra Sun or Ultra Moon. It doubles the game's speed too, and only takes on the game version it was tested on. **Zakuro mods folder** opens the game's mods folder (laid out as for Luma3DS and Azahar); cheats are in Zakuro's menu.
- **Saves** are the same as with the emulators, so a game can switch between 3DSrecomp+Zakuro and AzaharPlus or Azahar and keep its save (tested both ways on Pokémon Y).
- **Known issues:** some cutscenes miss textures or draw the sky wrong. Zakuro's mail and similar extra data aren't carried between engines, as with the emulators.

- **Looks:** each game has a drawn theme of its region (Kalos, Hoenn or Alola) in its own colors. Its wallpapers can come from your own play: while a 3DS game runs with its window in front, the launcher keeps a few pictures of the top screen, leaving out menus and dark or repeated ones, and shows one of them at random. They stay on your PC. Settings > Wallpapers switches to the drawn scenes instead, or gives any game a picture of your own.
